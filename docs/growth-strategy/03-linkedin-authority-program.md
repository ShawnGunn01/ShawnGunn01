# LinkedIn Authority Program: Content & Engagement Strategy

## Overview

Founder-led LinkedIn strategy to generate 25+ qualified leads per month through thought-leadership content, strategic relationship building, and permission-based engagement. Execution: 2 posts/week, 20 min daily engagement, curated 50-person relationship strategy.

## Four Editorial Pillars (Decision Categories)

**Pillar 1: GROW** (GTM, positioning, revenue decisions)
- Audience: VP Revenue, CMO, CEO at scaling companies
- Topics: GTM inflection, customer acquisition, revenue architecture, unit economics, board pressures
- Frequency: 3 posts/month

**Pillar 2: TRANSACT** (M&A, strategic alternatives, exits)
- Audience: CEO, founder, board chair, PE operator
- Topics: M&A strategy, founder exit timing, strategic alternatives, acquisition integration, portfolio consolidation
- Frequency: 3 posts/month

**Pillar 3: TRANSFORM** (Turnaround, restructuring, crises)
- Audience: CEO, newly hired CFO/COO, turnaround boards
- Topics: Turnaround playbooks, team restructuring, cost architecture, board dynamics, organizational health
- Frequency: 3 posts/month

**Pillar 4: CAPITALIZE** (Fundraising, investor relations, capital strategy)
- Audience: CFO, investor relations lead, CEO in fundraising mode
- Topics: Capital strategy, Series B/C/D dynamics, investor narrative, financial modeling, secondary sales
- Frequency: 3 posts/month

## Three Content Franchises

### Franchise 1: "The Decision"
**Format**: Narrative case study (600–800 words)  
**Cadence**: 1 per week (one per pillar per month)  
**Structure**:
- Anecdote or trigger moment
- Mistake/lesson learned
- Decision framework
- Outcome
- Implicit ask: "What decision are you navigating?"

**Example Angle**: "Three exits I've advised: founder sold at $8M without optimizing; founder negotiated $40M with prep. Here's why timing and positioning matter."

### Franchise 2: "Contrarian Note"
**Format**: Short, provocative take (3–5 paragraphs, 300–400 words)  
**Cadence**: 1 per week  
**Structure**:
- Bold contrarian claim ("Most founders overvalue their companies")
- Explain why conventional wisdom is incomplete
- Specific example or data point
- Implication for decision-makers
- CTA: "Do you agree or disagree?"

**Example Angle**: "Everyone tells Series B founders to hire a VP Sales. No one says: 'The right sales hire depends on your GTM strategy.' Most make the wrong hire because they haven't decided architecture first."

### Franchise 3: "The Read"
**Format**: Curated link + take (2–3 paragraphs, 200–300 words)  
**Cadence**: 2 per week (Monday + Thursday)  
**Structure**:
- Share relevant article/research
- Why it matters to [pillar] decision-makers
- Your take or counterpoint
- Implicit ask: "Thoughts?"

**Example Angle**: "This McKinsey on post-acquisition integration: 60% of acquisitions destroy value. So why? Most acquirers treat integration as an afterthought. How many board conversations start with 'integration plan' on day 1?"

**Title**: "Why Three CEOs Made Different GTM Choices—And Why Timing Matters"
**Pillar**: GROW

I've advised three founders at exactly the same stage: $8M ARR, Series B just closed, product-market fit locked. Their GTM decisions over the next 12 months determined $20M+ in value.

**Founder C** half-committed: hired VP Sales, half-heartedly explored GTM architecture, ended up with conflicting strategies for 8 months. Eventually aligned, but lost a year of momentum.

The pattern I've seen 40+ times: **GTM inflection moments are architecture decisions first, hiring decisions second.** Most founders treat it backwards.

**The question isn't "How fast do we grow?" It's "How do we want to grow?"** Architecture → Org → Execution.

### Post 2: TRANSACT Exit Strategy
**Title**: "Why Founder Exits Fail: The $15M Mistake I See Most"
**Pillar**: TRANSACT

I've advised 12 founders through exits. Seven of them left $5M–$40M on the table by not having a decision-ready answer to this question:

**"What am I optimizing for: liquidity, downside protection, upside capture, or optionality?"**

Most founder says: "I don't know—tell me what it's worth."

Wrong question. Before valuation, before process, before buyer meetings, that clarity changes everything.

**Founder A** optimized for liquidity: wanted to move on to next venture. Sold at $25M (undervalued at $35M+), but got deal done in 4 months, kept 100% focus on current business during process. Worth it for him.

**Founder B** optimized for upside capture: wanted max dollars if strategic buyer was serious. Opened door to 3 buyers, led with valuation expectations ($60M), brought process to 9 months, almost lost deal twice, sold at $55M. Worth the complexity for her.

**Founder C** optimized for optionality: wanted flexibility on staying post-sale. Structured earn-out heavily, took $30M upfront, stayed as COO for 2 years, made additional $12M on earnout. That structure only works if you're clear on it upfront.

**The mistake**: Going into process without this framework. Everything—buyer targeting, negotiation strategy, timeline, terms—flows from what you're actually optimizing for.

What's your current optimization? (And do you have the board/investor alignment on it?)

### Post 3: TRANSFORM Turnaround Blueprint
**Title**: "The Turnaround Playbook: Why Most Get It Wrong in Month 1"
**Pillar**: TRANSFORM

Walked into three turnarounds last year. Same problem each time: **Boards and new CEOs attack the problem in the wrong order.**

**The pattern**:
- Board says: "We need to cut costs, cut heads, refocus on cash"
- New CEO blasts in, cuts 20%, tightens metrics
- 60 days in: Top talent leaves, remaining team demoralized, customer churn accelerates
- 90 days in: Chaos, turnover, value destruction

**Here's what works instead:**

**Month 1**: Diagnosis, not action
- Map the business across 4 dimensions: Unit economics, customer health, team capability, cash runway
- Surface the real bottleneck (rarely where the board thinks it is)
- Build team trust through transparency

**Month 2**: Decision-ready
- Make the hard structural decisions (not tactical cuts): Do we keep/fix/exit business units? Do we reposition product? Do we restructure org or exec team?
- Communicate decisions so people can make their own choices (stay or go)

**Month 3**: Execution
- Execute decisions with discipline
- Track lead/lagging metrics
- Make course corrections

**The key**: Turnarounds are multi-dimensional. Most boards want to skip diagnosis and jump to execution. That's where value dies.

Have you been in a turnaround that moved too fast in month 1?

### Post 4: CAPITALIZE Fundraising Framework
**Title**: "Why Your Fundraising Strategy Fails Before Your First Investor Meeting"
**Pillar**: CAPITALIZE

I've watched three Series B companies raise capital. Two took 8 months, burned board cycles, accepted lower valuations. One took 6 weeks, got multiple offers at premium valuation.

**The difference wasn't the product or traction. It was strategy.**

The slow ones started with: "We're raising. Let me pitch everyone."

The fast one started with: "What's the narrative? Who cares? How do we position defensively?"

**That company did this**:
- Mapped institutional preferences (which firms back their sector, stage, model)
- Built the financial model *before* pitching (most founders don't; investors see raw numbers)
- Positioned the narrative defensively (explained headwinds + plan to address)
- Targeted 3 lead candidates with customized info
- Generated competitive tension
- Closed lead in 4 weeks

**The mistaken approach**: Spreadsheet of 100 VCs, mass pitch deck, hope for the best.

**The strategic approach**: 10 VCs, customized narrative, financial model ready, competitive tension generated.

Before you fundraise again, ask: "Do I have a capital strategy, or just a pitch?"

## Daily 20-Minute Engagement Routine

**Timing**: Schedule daily between 2–3pm (peak LinkedIn activity)  
**Frequency**: Monday–Friday, 20 minutes/day

### Engagement Workflow (20 min total)

**Minutes 1–3: Scan Relevant Hashtags & Posts**
- Search: #Series, #Fundraising, #M&A, #Startup, #CEO, #Founder
- Skim top 5 posts from each category
- Identify 3–5 posts to comment on

**Minutes 4–15: Comment Strategically (3 comments)**
- **Comment 1** (3 min): On a GROW-related post
  - Add nuanced take or question that shows expertise
  - Example: "This assumes GTM model is already clear. Most companies I work with skip that step—then hire the wrong sales person."
  - No self-promotion; just insight
  
- **Comment 2** (3 min): On a TRANSACT-related post
  - Same approach: expertise, contrarian nuance, genuine reaction
  
- **Comment 3** (3 min): On a TRANSFORM or CAPITALIZE post
  - Example: "The turnaround fails when boards push for speed. The companies that recovered slowed down, diagnosed, then moved fast with clarity."

**Minutes 16–20: Profile Engagement (50-person tier strategy)**
- Spend 5 minutes engaging with 3–5 profiles from your "50-person strategic relationship list" (see below)
- Actions: Like their recent post, thoughtful comment, or visit profile without interaction
- Rotate through list so each person gets 1–2 touches/week from you

### Comment Quality Checklist
- ✓ Adds new insight or nuance (not repeat of post)
- ✓ Shows decision-maker perspective (not student perspective)
- ✓ Genuine reaction (not performative)
- ✓ No hard CTA or self-promotion
- ✓ 2–3 sentences max
- ✓ Professional tone with personality

## Permission-Based Conversation System

**Golden Rule**: Never pitch on LinkedIn. Always build permission first.

### Permission Levels (Build from 1 → 2 → 3)

**Level 1: Passive Awareness**
- They see your content (posts, engagement)
- They follow you
- No interaction yet

**Action**: Maintain 20-min daily engagement; post 2x/week; they see your POV

**Level 2: Active Engagement**
- They like, comment on, or share your content
- You've commented on their content 2–3 times
- Relationship is now mutual

**Action**: Send thoughtful DM acknowledging engagement; reference specific post they engaged with; ask a genuine question (not a pitch)

**Level 3: Conversation Permission**
- You've had 1+ DM exchanges (back and forth)
- They've shown interest in your expertise (asked a question, shared a challenge)
- Timing is right for a call offer

**Action**: "I'm curious about [specific challenge they mentioned]. Would a 20-min call be useful? No pitch—just wanted to explore your thinking."

### DM Sequence (Permission-Building)

**DM 1** (after 2–3 content engagements from them):
"[Name], enjoyed your post on [topic]. Haven't seen many people articulate that angle. Quick question: [genuine curiosity question, no pitch]"

**DM 2** (if they reply):
"Thanks for that insight. That confirms what I'm seeing with [example company]. Wondering if [relevant follow-up question specific to their challenge]?"

**DM 3** (if conversation continues):
"This is helpful. Would a 20-min call make sense to explore further? No pitch—genuinely curious about your approach to [their challenge]."

## DM Sequences for Warm Engagers

### Sequence A: Content to Call (For active engagers)
- DM 1: Reference their engagement, ask genuine question
- DM 2: Share relevant insight from your network
- DM 3: Suggest call if conversation warrants

### Sequence B: Cold to Warm (For target ICP)
- DM 1: Reference their content + one specific insight
- DM 2: Wait for reply (don't DM again if no response within 1 week)
- DM 3: If they engage, suggest call

### Sequence C: Nurture (For engaged contacts, not yet ready to meet)
- DM monthly: Share relevant insight related to their current challenge
- Use first-person examples: "I just helped [company] navigate [challenge] you mentioned"
- Keep permission-based: never pitch, always educate first

## Performance Thresholds

### Lagging Metrics (Monthly)
- Inbound inquiries: Target 15–20 → 8–10 MQL
- SQL from LinkedIn: Target 3–5
- Calls booked: Target 2–4
- Closed clients from LinkedIn: Target 0–1/month

### Leading Metrics (Weekly)
- Post engagement rate: Target 3–5% (comments + likes / impressions)
- Profile visits: Target 50–100/month
- DM conversations started: Target 3–5 initiated
- Comment quality score: Ensure expertise shown in every comment
- Engagement consistency: 5/5 days posting 2x/week + daily engagement

### Quality Thresholds
- Minimum engagement rate: 2% (posts <2% engagement indicate content-market misalignment)
- Minimum reply rate on DMs: 30% (DM 1s should get 30% replies; if lower, adjust template)
- Minimum call-booking rate: 25% (of conversations that reach DM 3, 25% should book calls)
